# Reading notes: noticing gaps during study

Source: `reading-passage.md`. This is an original, synthetic classroom passage, not a published study. It identifies no author, publication date, or external references.

## Main claim

Trying to recall an idea without looking at notes can reveal gaps that rereading alone may hide. Feedback matters: an attempted answer should be compared with the source before it is retained.

## Classroom observation

A teacher observed two existing groups of five learners each. Both groups studied the same ten definitions for fifteen minutes, followed by an immediate ten-question quiz.

| Group | Study activity | Average correct answers out of 10 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Rereading | 6 |
| B | Recall followed by checking | 8 |

Group B had the higher observed average: 8 out of 10, compared with 6 out of 10 in Group A.

## Limitations

- The groups were not randomly assigned.
- This was one small classroom observation.
- There was no delayed test.
- The observation does not establish that the activity caused the difference or that the difference persists over time.

## Practical suggestion in the passage

After studying, attempt a short explanation from memory, check it against the notes, and revise any mistakes.
